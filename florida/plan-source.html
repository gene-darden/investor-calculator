The thesis. The $600k-and-up single-family and townhome market from Santa Rosa Beach to Panama City is large (roughly $3.4 billion a year EST), growing (30A closings up about 35% year to date, Panama City Beach up about 19% HARD), and dominated by luxury-lifestyle teams that do not speak the investor's language. The $600k to $2M investor buyer who wants the numbers run, the tax engine explained, and the rules de-risked is underserved. That is the lane.

It is a real stretch for a first full year in a market where Gene has a license, a CPAR membership, a Panama City Beach listing, and a YouTube channel, but no established 30A presence. The plan treats $1M as the stretch goal and $600k as the floor (24 sides), with the pipeline and activity math built for the stretch.

The plan builds the brand "The Coastal Blueprint" around three things no competitor is doing together: (1) a free, interactive beach-home pro forma with after-tax returns, (2) plain-English teaching on the short-term rental tax rules, cost segregation and 100% bonus depreciation, and 1031 exchanges, and (3) a verified-actuals standard: every property is presented with trailing-12 rental actuals, not projections.

4.2 The deal screen (what "a good deal" means here)

Every property Gene presents to an investor passes through the same screen, in this order. The screen is the product. Publish it; it becomes the brand.

  1. Can it legally rent? County/city STR certificate eligibility (Walton VRC, PCB Ord. 1632), zoning, HOA covenants, DBPR license path. If no, it is a second home, not an investment. Underwrite accordingly.
  2. What did it actually gross? Trailing-12 owner statements or PM actuals. No actuals: use AirDNA/Rabbu comps by bedroom count and distance to beach, then haircut 15%.
  3. What does it cost to carry? Property tax at the reassessed purchase price (not the seller's capped bill), wind and flood insurance quotes in hand before the inspection period ends, HOA, management at 20 to 25%, utilities, 5% to 8% maintenance and supplies, 3% to 5% capex reserve.
  4. What does the debt look like? Second-home (10% down, lower rate, personal use required) versus DSCR (20 to 25% down, 1.0 to 1.25x coverage on AirDNA or actuals) versus cash. Jumbo thresholds matter above the 2026 conforming limit.
  5. What is the after-tax year-one? Cost-seg estimate (20 to 35% of depreciable basis into 5/7/15-year property) with 100% bonus depreciation; STR material-participation test feasibility for this buyer; marginal bracket; the resulting cash tax savings. Section 10 has the method.
  6. What is the five-year hold? Appreciation at a conservative 3% to 4%, principal paydown, exit at a cap rate 50 bps above today's, selling cost 6%, depreciation recapture at 25%, NIIT 3.8%, and the 1031 option. Internal rate of return on all four engines.
  7. What is the downside? Occupancy −15%, ADR −10%, insurance +25%, rate +100 bps. Does it still carry? Can the buyer fund a negative year without stress?
Gene's rule of thumb for the corridor (publish it): Buy gross rent at 7% or better of price with verified actuals, insure before you inspect, underwrite the tax engine as a bonus and never as the reason, and only buy what you would be happy to own empty for a season.

4.3 Where "good deals" surface

  • Overpriced-and-sitting luxury listings at 120+ DOM with two or more price cuts (search Flexmls weekly). Sellers are often out-of-state owners tired of management; a clean DSCR or cash offer at a 7% gross yield wins.
  • Expired and withdrawn listings $800k+ in Walton and Bay from the last 24 months. Owners already decided to sell once.
  • Out-of-state owners with 10+ years of ownership (property-appraiser export). Large embedded gains, aging owners, 1031 or step-up conversations.
  • New construction in PCB West End and west 30A from builders holding specs: negotiate rate buydowns and furnishings packages; new build means lower insurance (2020+ code), full cost-seg basis, and warranty.
  • Estates and divorces via Walton and Bay probate filings and local attorneys.
  • PM partner "owner wants out" lists. Every property manager knows which owners are tired. A referral agreement with two 30A and two PCB managers is a listing pipeline.

10.1 The short-term rental rule (the "STR loophole")

  • Statute/reg: Treas. Reg. §1.469-1T(e)(3). An activity is not a "rental activity" if the average guest stay is 7 days or less, or 30 days or less with significant personal services. If it is not a rental activity, the automatic passive rule does not apply. If the owner materially participates, losses are non-passive and offset W-2 and business income.
  • Material participation tests used most (§1.469-5T(a)): 500+ hours; "substantially all" of the work; or 100+ hours and more than any other individual (including the cleaner and the property manager). Spouse's hours count. Keep a contemporaneous log. Investor-type hours (reading statements) do not count. Full-service PM arrangements usually defeat the 100-hour test; self-managed or co-hosted setups pass it.
  • What it is not: a ≤7-day STR is not eligible for the $25,000 active-participation allowance (that is for rental real estate only), and Real Estate Professional Status (750 hours) is irrelevant to it.
  • Schedule E vs C: short stays alone do not trigger self-employment tax. SE tax (15.3%) applies only with substantial hotel-like services (daily cleaning during the stay, meals, concierge). Turnover cleaning, linens, Wi-Fi and utilities are not substantial. Most loophole filers stay on Schedule E.
  • Loss cap: §461(l) excess business loss limit, made permanent by the 2025 tax act: roughly $313k single / $626k joint for 2025, indexed for 2026 (VERIFY). Larger cost-seg losses carry forward as NOLs.

10.2 Bonus depreciation and cost segregation

  • 100% bonus depreciation is permanent under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (P.L. 119-21, July 4, 2025) for qualified property (recovery period ≤20 years) acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025. Property under a binding contract before January 20, 2025 stays on the old phase-down (40% in 2025).
  • Cost segregation reclassifies 20 to 40% of depreciable basis (furnished STRs typically 22 to 35%) into 5, 7 and 15-year property: furniture, appliances, carpet, decorative lighting, pools, decks, driveways, landscaping, fencing. All of it is bonus-eligible in year one.
  • Study cost: engineering-based $5,000 to $15,000; virtual/flat-fee $2,000 to $3,500. Partners: KBKG, Engineered Tax Services, RE Cost Seg.
  • Recapture: the reclassified 5/7/15-year property is §1245 property; on sale that depreciation is recaptured at ordinary rates, not the 25% rate. Plan the exit (hold, 1031, or step-up at death).
  • 27.5 vs 39 years: residential rental (27.5) requires ≥80% of rents from dwelling units; §168(e)(2)(A)(ii)(I) excludes units in a hotel or "other establishment" where more than half the units are transient (<30 days). Practitioners split on whether a single-family STR is an "establishment." Example on $640k building basis: ~$23.3k/yr at 27.5 vs ~$16.4k/yr at 39. Get the CPA's written position.

10.4 Section 1031 exchanges

  • Timelines: identify replacement property within 45 days; close within 180 days (or the return due date including extensions if earlier). Both clocks run from the relinquished closing. Calendar days.
  • Identification: 3-property rule, 200% rule, or 95% rule. A Qualified Intermediary is required; the agent, attorney, or CPA from the prior 2 years is a disqualified person. QI fees ~$750 to $1,500 forward; $2,500 to $5,000+ reverse/improvement; reverse all-in often $7,000 to $15,000+.
  • Reverse exchange: Rev. Proc. 2000-37; an Exchange Accommodation Titleholder parks the property under a QEAA within 5 business days; same 45/180 limits.
  • Vacation homes "held for investment": Rev. Proc. 2008-16 safe harbor: for each of the two 12-month periods before (relinquished) and after (replacement) the exchange, rent at fair value ≥14 days and keep personal use ≤ the greater of 14 days or 10% of rented days. Outside the safe harbor, facts and circumstances (Moore v. Commissioner risk).
  • §121 + §1031: Rev. Proc. 2005-14 allows the $250k/$500k residence exclusion plus deferral on a converted residence; §121(d)(10): no §121 exclusion within 5 years of acquiring property in a 1031; §121(b)(5) prorates for non-qualified (rental) use.

10.5 Personal use, QBI, and the rest

  • §280A personal use: the home is a "residence" if personal use exceeds the greater of 14 days or 10% of fair-rental days. Then expenses are allocated and rental deductions are capped at rental income (excess carries forward). Repair days do not count as personal use; family and below-market use do. Fewer than 15 rental days: income excluded, no deductions (§280A(g)). Bolton allocation (interest and taxes over 365 days) usually beats the IRS method.
  • QBI (§199A): 20% deduction made permanent; 2026 thresholds $201,750 single / $403,500 joint with wider phase-ins. Rentals qualify as a §162 business or under the Rev. Proc. 2019-38 safe harbor (250+ hours of rental services by owner, employees or contractors; separate books; logs). The safe harbor excludes property used as a residence under §280A(d), so heavy personal use knocks it out.
  • Passive rules for non-STR rentals: $25,000 allowance phases out $1 per $2 of MAGI above $100k, gone at $150k; suspended losses release on full disposition. REPS: 750+ hours and more than half of personal services in real property businesses, plus material participation.
  • Sale taxes (2026): unrecaptured §1250 gain at up to 25%; §1245 recapture at ordinary rates; remaining gain at 15%/20% (20% above $583,400 single / $613,700 joint); NIIT 3.8% above $200k/$250k MAGI (not indexed); income from a materially participated trade or business is generally not NII (confirm). 35% bracket starts at $256,226 single / $512,451 joint; 37% above $640,600 / $768,700.
  • Estate: §1014 step-up at death unchanged; exemption $15M per person from January 1, 2026, indexed. Step-up erases deferred gain and all recapture: the "buy, borrow, 1031, die" ladder.
  • SALT and mortgage interest: SALT cap $40,400 in 2026 (phases down above $500k MAGI); property tax on a rental is a Schedule E expense, not SALT; on a personal second home it is SALT. $750k acquisition-debt interest cap is permanent and covers primary plus one second home; interest on a rental allocates to Schedule E and is not capped.
  • Opportunity Zones: Bay County has three current tracts, all in Panama City (tracts 16, 18, 20) through 2028; none on the beach or 30A. OZ 2.0 nominations were due July to September 2026 with new tracts effective January 1, 2027; whether any new Bay County tracts were nominated is unverified. Not a beach-home strategy.
