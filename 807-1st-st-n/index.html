The bottom line

The owner is talking about around $800,000. On the evidence I can verify today, that is the top of the supportable range, not the middle of it. I put a defensible value at $650,000 to $800,000, most likely near $725,000, and I would open lower than that and let the walk-through earn any move up.

The building is a roughly 5,850 sq ft, closed restaurant on 0.38 acre. A buyer pays for the corner, the hospital traffic and the location. What the building itself is worth depends on three things we will see at 9:30: how much of the kitchen is intact, what shape the roof and HVAC are in, and whether the parking and zoning fit the use.

Three ways to look at value

1. Price per square foot

$585K – $820K
  • The one nearby retail comp I found is asking about $117 per sq ft.
  • Applying $100 to $140 per sq ft to 5,848 sq ft gives this range.
  • The owner's $800K works out to $137 per sq ft, near the top.

2. Income if leased

$615K – $1.03M
  • Assumes a triple-net rent of $10 to $15 per sq ft and an 8.5% to 9.5% cap rate. Both are my assumptions, not verified market data.
  • Midpoint: $73,100 NOI at a 9% cap is about $812K.
  • This is the test that matters if the plan is to hold and lease.

3. Land plus building

$2.1M per acre
  • At $800K the land alone is priced at about $48 per sq ft of lot.
  • That is a high land number for a 0.38 acre lot, so the building has to carry real value.
  • If the structure is tired, the deal rests on the location, not the improvements.

Recommended offer strategy
  • Open at $680,000. That is about $116 per sq ft, in line with the one retail comp I found, and it leaves room to move.
  • Target $725,000. About $124 per sq ft, the middle of the supportable range.
  • Do not go past $800,000 unless the walk-through shows a good roof, working HVAC and a kitchen that conveys, and a rent comp supports the middle income case.
  • Move before it lists. The owner said it is not yet listed and others are interested. A clean, quick offer with a short due-diligence period is worth real money to him. Use that as the reason to hold the price down, not to raise it.
  • Offer to buy as-is, subject to diligence. Ask for 30 days of due diligence and a 30 to 45 day close.
  • Price risk to watch: he first said "sell or lease." If he leans toward leasing, a lease with a purchase option is a lower-cash way in.
What to check at the 9:30 showing
  • Roof: age, leaks, ponding, any warranty. This is the biggest single repair risk on a building this size.
  • HVAC: number of units, age and condition. Ask for service records.
  • Kitchen: does the hood, fire suppression, walk-in cooler, grease trap and gas service stay with the building? Take photos and a list.
  • Parking and access: count the spaces, check curb cuts onto 1st St N, and look at cross-access with neighbors.
  • Zoning and permitted uses: confirm with the City of Alabaster that restaurant, retail or your intended use is allowed without a variance.
  • Condition inside: water stains, floors, restrooms, ADA access, electrical panel size.
  • Environmental: restaurants are low risk, but ask about any prior gas station or dry-cleaner use on the lot.
  • Paperwork to request from the owner: last survey, title policy, tax bills, utility bills and any lease or tenant history.
Open questions that move the price
  • Year builtOlder than about 25 years usually means roof, HVAC and plumbing money is coming. Budget it into the offer.
  • Last saleWhat the owner paid, and when. It tells you how firm $800K really is.
  • TaxesShelby County bill for the last two years. It sets the true carry cost.
  • Intended useHold and lease, owner-occupy, or redevelop? The answer changes which of the three value methods you should trust.
  • Flood and utilitiesConfirm the FEMA zone and that water, sewer and gas are all on site.
